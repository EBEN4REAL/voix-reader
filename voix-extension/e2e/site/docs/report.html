Annual Listening Report

Listening to documents is becoming a normal part of work. People review reports while commuting, and they catch up on research while cooking dinner.

A good reader has to cope with every kind of file. Web pages are only the beginning. Reports arrive as PDFs, notes live in shared documents, and old records are often scanned images.

Dr. Rivera, who led the study, put it simply. The tool should read the document and nothing else: no page numbers, no running titles, and no web addresses printed in the margins.

This section exists to make the report long enough to span more than one page, so that headers and footers repeat. Repetition is how the reader recognises page furniture and leaves it out.
